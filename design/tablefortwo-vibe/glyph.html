The notched glyph.

Your screenshot, decoded: there are no chair dots in the final mark at all. Two dots sit exactly on the table’s edge at 3 and 9 o’clock and are knocked out of the circle. What survives is two horizontal semicircular arcs — the top and bottom of a table for two, with the two seats present only as absence. That’s the logo: negative space is the “for two.” This page’s tab icon is already the corrected version, in gold on black.

Why this is stronger than my dot version: dots-on-a-ring reads “loading / orbit / generic tech.” The notched version reads as one confident gesture at 16px, can’t be mistaken for anyone else’s mark, and has a story a judge can repeat in one sentence — “it’s a table with the two seats cut out.”

What it is not

  • Not a “C”, not a loading spinner — the two gaps, symmetric on the horizontal axis, are what separate it. Never rotate it 90°: vertical gaps would read as a mouth / parentheses.
  • Never add the dots back. The absence is the idea.
  • Never outline the tile in the mark itself — the squircle is the OS’s job.
