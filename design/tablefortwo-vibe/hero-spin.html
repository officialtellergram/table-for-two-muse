Reading the motion

The spin = a table, from above, being watched

  • Main ring is the table-top; the two dots are the two chairs — as it turns, the “for two” orbits the wordmark instead of sitting in the tagline (where it wrongly capped the party size).
  • The cream chair vs gold chair: you and the restaurant, meeting on the ring.
  • A short bright arc rides the ring — the next drop, coming around.

The counter-spin = the drop clock

  • 60 faint ticks, longer on the hour, rotating the other way — reservations release on a clock, and the brand literally keeps time behind the logo.
  • The lone orbit dot is the cancellation that just opened up. It’s the radar, in one dot.
  • Nothing here needs halftone: flat gold line on ink is cheaper to animate, sharper at every size, and unmistakably yours.

Entrance (the “spin-in” you referenced)

  • On load in production: glyph starts at 0.82 scale, −70° rotation, 0 opacity and settles into the idle spin over ~1.4s ease-out — it should feel like a plate being set down, not a loading spinner.
  • Wordmark letters then rise 8px, staggered 30ms; the gold period pulses last. Total intro ≤1.8s, skippable on tap, never replayed within a session.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: static glyph, no pulse. Respected in this prototype too.
